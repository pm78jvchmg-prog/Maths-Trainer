# C21-l3: Forces & Newton's Laws: Connected Particles on Slopes

Status: done
Branch: `claude/roadmap-c21-forces-l3-c12bzx`

Level `fo-l3` of course `forces`, 5 lessons (`fo-l3-incline`, `fo-l3-rough`,
`fo-l3-peg`, `fo-l3-slack`, `fo-l3-together`) and a 15-question level check,
17 new `force-*` generators.

- One model, `rigMotion`, covers every set-up: particle A on one face and B on
  another, joined over a smooth pulley or peg. A face is an `Angle`, so a
  slope with B hanging (`DROP`), two rough slopes back to back, and a rough
  table (`FLAT`) with a slope beyond its edge are the same code. `rigSvg` draws
  them at their true angles, with each angle marked at the foot of its face.
- Every final answer (acceleration, tension, speed, distance, balancing mass)
  is whole or a tenth with g = 9.8. The tidy cases are sparse (the total mass
  is nearly always a multiple of 7), so draws come from catalogues built once
  on first use rather than from a rejection loop. Intermediate forces are exact
  to at most three places.
- The `forces` kind is asked in two generators: `force-incline-pick` (the
  forces on A on a slope, or on B hanging) and `force-incline-fill` (the
  magnitudes on A). Tension and friction both point up a slope when A slides
  down, and two arrows cannot share a direction, so both only draw A being
  pulled up (or held from being pulled up). The fill diagram uses the 3-4-5
  slope, 36.9 degrees, the only exact angle the widget can draw.
- Kinematics' v^2 = u^2 + 2as (`kn-l2-more`) is used for the slack string,
  not taught again.
- `forces.test.ts` recomputes every answer by hand through `Math.atan2`, and
  grades both diagrams through `toggleForce`, `startSession` and `submit`.
  Two mutations (friction dropped from the tension, friction dropped from the
  pick answer) were confirmed to turn it red.
- Full suite 55,213 tests, all passing, before the final layout fixes.
