# C9-l8: Algebraic Fractions: Factorising to Simplify

Status: done
Branch: `claude/under30-algebraic-fractions-u84jc5`

Level `af-l8` in course `algebraic-fractions`, 5 lessons (Common Factors
First, Differences of Two Squares, Quadratics with a Number in Front,
Grouping and Cubics, Simplify, Then Combine), a level check of 15 questions,
20 new generators (`af8-hcf-tiles` to `af8-combo-add-tree`) in
`generators/fractionsLevel8.ts`, 5 of them with a `+choice` form.

- Level 1 already cancels brackets in view, a number out of a linear top,
  x^2 - s^2 and a reversed bracket. This level is the factorising that comes
  first: a number and a power of x together, A^2x^2 - B^2 (and one behind a
  common factor), ax^2 + bx + c by the ac method, cubics by a common x or by
  grouping, then simplifying before multiplying, dividing or adding.
- Every line is a `Fac` (a number, a power of x, brackets ax + b with nothing
  common to a and b); the question is the product multiplied out and the
  answer is what `cancelled` leaves, so every result and every value at a
  hole is exact by construction. Options, branches, step banks and tile banks
  drop any slip worth the same as the thing it stands in for (`byValue`),
  since flipping both halves of a difference of two squares changes nothing.
- Widgets: tiles (common factor, differences of two squares, ac, cubics,
  every "simplify fully" as top and bottom), tree (the ac method; simplify
  each then add), steps (a difference of two squares simplified line by
  line, grouping, dividing), flow (common factors; the ac method then
  cancel), choice, and typed whole numbers (the value at a hole at 0, at a
  fractional x and at a cubic's root; a value after simplifying).
- No generator declares `source`, `integrand` or `limits` (nothing is
  calculus). `fractionsLevel8.test.ts` reads only the screen: factorisations
  multiply back to the line shown, simplified forms equal the fraction with
  no common root left, the ac pair multiplies to ac and adds to b, every line
  of a steps slide equals the one it started from, and each value at a hole
  is the numerical limit of the fraction there.
